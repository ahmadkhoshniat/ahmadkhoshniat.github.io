About

I am a PhD candidate in Visual and Performing Arts at The University of Texas at Dallas whose research and creative practice focus on cinema and moving-image media. As a filmmaker and practitioner-researcher, I explore cinematic form and politics, transnational political image, Iranian art cinema, experimental animation, political aesthetics, intercultural cinema, and practice-as-research. Working across theory and filmmaking practice, I investigate how aesthetic and cinematic strategies can contribute to new forms of political expression.

Current Research

Creating a Transnational Political Image Through Experimental Animation: Bridging Resistant Strategies of Iranian Art Cinema with Anti-hegemonic Aesthetics

My doctoral research investigates how a political image can be produced through experimental animation. The project establishes a dialogue between the resistant cinematic strategies of Iranian art cinema—particularly in the works of Abbas Kiarostami, Jafar Panahi, and Mohsen Makhmalbaf—and anti-hegemonic aesthetic theories associated by Jacques Rancière, Laura U. Marks, and Hamid Naficy. By examining how political meaning emerges through form, perception, and sensory experience rather than explicit political content, the research develops a framework for creating political cinematic images. Conducted through a Practice-as-Research methodology, the project culminates in the production of an experimental animated film that applies and tests these theoretical and cinematic insights in practice.
